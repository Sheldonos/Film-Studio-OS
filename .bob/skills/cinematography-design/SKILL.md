# Cinematography Design

## When to use
Translate dramatic intent into camera grammar, shot size, lens/perspective, movement, composition, depth and lighting plans.

## Procedure
1. Load director intention, blocking/geography, world/style bible, continuity state, delivery aspect/color constraints and production strategy.
2. Choose shot size, camera height/distance and lens/perspective to control intimacy, power, spatial compression, distortion and information—not from a generic “cinematic” preset.
3. Motivate camera movement from subject movement, reveal, emotional transition or viewpoint; prefer static frames when movement adds no narrative value.
4. Design depth with foreground/midground/background, parallax and focus strategy while protecting generation/animation feasibility.
5. Define motivated key/fill/backlight/environment sources, contrast and color-temperature relationships that can persist across coverage.
6. Establish sequence grammar and exceptions; a deliberate visual rupture needs a dramatic reason and continuity plan.
7. Translate into CameraSpec/LightingSpec plus model-neutral constraints; provider-specific syntax is compiled later.

## Required behavior
- Preserve approved creative intent and exact upstream version lineage.
- Separate verified evidence, inference and unknowns.
- Never let authoring stand in for independent final review.
- Never initiate a paid/external action without explicit authorization.
