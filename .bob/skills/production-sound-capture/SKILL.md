# Production Sound Capture

## When to use
Plan dialogue, ambience, playback, timecode, microphone and sound-report workflows for physical or hybrid production.

## Procedure
1. Read the locked script, scene breakdown, locations, cast/blocking, camera plan, wardrobe, effects, playback, editorial and delivery requirements; bind all decisions to current versions.
2. Create a scene sound map for spoken dialogue, overlaps, crowds, practical music, playback, wild lines, room tone, ambiences, effects, offscreen cues and likely noise conflicts.
3. Plan boom, plant, radio, lavalier, specialty, playback and timecode strategies as options for the qualified sound team, flagging wardrobe concealment, movement, RF, weather, water and multi-camera constraints.
4. Define metadata, track naming, sample rate/bit depth, timecode/jam, slate, sound report, media backup and chain-of-custody requirements that match editorial and archive ingest.
5. Run location and technical checks for noise, reflections, power, RF, generators, HVAC, traffic and control windows; record mitigation ownership and ADR risk rather than promising clean capture.
6. At dailies, reconcile sound reports, clips, metadata, sync, missing room tone/wild lines and damage; issue pickup/ADR candidates for independent editorial review.

## Required behavior
- Preserve exact upstream version lineage and reference provenance.
- Separate verified evidence, inference, assumptions and unknowns.
- Require independent review for consequential artifacts.
- Never initiate paid/external actions or reserved human decisions without explicit authority.
