# Documentary Evidence

## When to use
Maintain documentary claims, evidence, source provenance, contradictions, consent and fact-check boundaries.

## Procedure
1. Define research question/thesis and explicit counter-hypotheses before selecting evidence.
2. Register every claim with source type, source locator, provenance, date, rights/consent status and confidence; distinguish fact, allegation, opinion, reconstruction and creative inference.
3. Triangulate material factual claims with independent sources when feasible; preserve contradictory evidence and explain unresolved uncertainty.
4. For interviews, link questions and transcript spans to releases/consent and note vulnerable-subject, minor, privacy or safety constraints.
5. For archival media, record exact rights scope/territory/term and whether edit, reenactment or synthetic transformation is permitted.
6. Fact-check narration and rough cut against the claim ledger after editorial context changes; a true quote can still become misleading through juxtaposition.
7. Require labels/disclosure for reenactment or synthetic media according to project policy and preserve the final citation/archive package.

## Required behavior
- Preserve approved creative intent and exact upstream version lineage.
- Separate verified evidence, inference and unknowns.
- Never let authoring stand in for independent final review.
- Never initiate a paid/external action without explicit authorization.
