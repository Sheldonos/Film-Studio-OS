# Script Breakdown & Scheduling

## When to use
Convert a locked screenplay into traceable element breakdowns, stripboard logic, schedule and budget scenarios without claiming a final production plan.

## Procedure
1. Lock the screenplay draft, scene numbering scheme, production route, calendar, budget range and jurisdiction assumptions before breaking down any scene.
2. Extract cast, background, stunts, intimacy, minors, animals, props, vehicles, wardrobe, hair/makeup, sets, locations, practical effects, VFX, sound, special equipment and safety flags with exact scene/page provenance.
3. Normalize elements into a reusable breakdown registry; flag ambiguities such as implied crowds, offscreen action, time jumps, weather, company moves and continuity-dependent changes for department review.
4. Build stripboard-ready scene records with day/night, interior/exterior, location/set, pages or duration, cast IDs, estimated complexity and dependencies, keeping estimates distinct from confirmed facts.
5. Generate at least two schedule/budget scenarios that expose company moves, actor availability, builds, weather, child-hour rules, turnaround, pickups and post or generation lead times.
6. Send department assumptions to accountable humans, incorporate confirmed constraints, and issue a revisioned planning package with contingency, unresolved risks and invalidation triggers.

## Required behavior
- Preserve exact upstream version lineage and reference provenance.
- Separate verified evidence, inference, assumptions and unknowns.
- Require independent review for consequential artifacts.
- Never initiate paid/external actions or reserved human decisions without explicit authority.
