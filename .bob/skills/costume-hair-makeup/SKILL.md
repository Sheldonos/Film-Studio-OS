# Costume, Hair & Makeup

## When to use
Design character appearance, costume plots, fittings and continuity states across story time, scenes and production methods.

## Procedure
1. Read the locked script, character arcs, period/world bible, cast requirements, scene breakdown, schedule, climate, movement, stunt, intimacy and effects needs before proposing looks.
2. Create a character-by-character design rationale covering silhouette, palette, fabric/material, wear, cultural meaning, status, body autonomy, hair, makeup, prosthetic and practical-versus-digital treatment.
3. Build a scene-linked costume and appearance plot with numbered looks, changes, duplicates, pre/post damage, aging, blood/dirt/wet states, quick changes and continuity photographs or generated reference needs.
4. Plan sourcing/build, fittings, accessibility, allergies, skin/hair care, modesty, mobility, stunt duplicates, cleaning, repair, sustainability and clearance, marking unknown performer data as pending.
5. Coordinate look tests under representative camera, lighting, color and movement conditions; compare physical or generated results against the approved character identity and world palette.
6. Publish versioned look packets, continuity cards and day-specific handoffs, with independent review and explicit human approval for performer-facing, cultural, medical or prosthetic decisions.

## Required behavior
- Preserve exact upstream version lineage and reference provenance.
- Separate verified evidence, inference, assumptions and unknowns.
- Require independent review for consequential artifacts.
- Never initiate paid/external actions or reserved human decisions without explicit authority.
